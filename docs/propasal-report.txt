1. Client Background
The National Emergency Response and Coordination Center (NERCC) is a government agency
established in 2021 under Sri Lanka’s Ministry of Health & Disaster Management. As a central
authority, NERCC oversees two separate operational arms: the 1990 Suwa Seriya Foundation
(ambulance service) and the Sri Lanka Fire Service Department (fire‐rescue).
Suwa Seriya Foundation began in 2016 with an initial fleet of 88 ambulances and has since
expanded to 297 units across all nine provinces, offering free pre-hospital emergency care with
an average response time of 15 minutes 32 seconds. Its 24/7 command center uses a
homegrown GPS tracking and Google-traffic routing system, and EMTs receive real-time
medical direction from on-call physicians.
The Sri Lanka Fire Service Department traces its roots to the 1898 Colombo Fire Brigade and
today comprises over twenty municipal brigades serving all provinces. Its fleet includes more
than 350 fire engines, ladder units, and specialized rescue vehicles stationed at fire stations
and sub-stations island-wide. Emergency calls (110 or 011-2422222) route through regional
control centers that dispatch crews for fire suppression, rescue, and hazardous-materials
response.
Despite these strengths, both services run independently, relying heavily on voice calls and
manual logs for incident intake and dispatch. Suwa Seriya and FSD lack integrated scheduling,
digital confirmation loops, real-time ETA broadcasting to callers, and unified analytics.
Historical incident records remain on paper, limiting after-action reviews and strategic planning.
By implementing our MERN-based Emergency Dispatch System—with digital intake forms,
geocoded confirmation loops, real-time GPS tracking, dynamic routing (traffic + weather),
offline-capable mobile reporting, integrated workforce management, and centralized
dashboards—NERCC will unify Suwa Seriya and Fire Service workflows, reduce call-to-dispatch
times, eliminate transcription errors, and equip leadership with actionable insights for
continuous improvement.

2. Problem and Motivations
2.1 Problem Statement
NERCC’s incident management process relies on manual call-taking and radio dispatch,
resulting in delayed response times, data-entry errors, and a lack of real-time situational
awareness.
2.2 Motivations
Adopting a digital Emergency Dispatch System yields clear benefits:
• Reduce call-to-dispatch latency by at least 50% through form-based intake and
automated routing.
• Improve data accuracy and completeness by enforcing structured fields and geocoding.
• Provide real-time map-based dashboards for dispatchers, integrating traffic and
weather layers.
• Send automated SMS/push notifications to citizens (ETA, unit arrival) and field crews
(assignments, reassignments).
• Enable post-incident analytics—response-time trends, resource utilization, incident
hotspots—to guide future staffing and vehicle procurement.
These improvements will enhance NERCC’s operational efficiency, strengthen citizen trust, and
lay the foundation for continuous performance optimization.

4. System Overview
The Emergency Dispatch System is a unified, two‐tier platform combining a robust web
application for dispatchers and administrators with a lightweight companion mobile app for
citizens and field responders. On the web side, call-takers and dispatchers access a centralized
console to intake incidents, triage by severity, allocate resources, monitor real-time vehicle
positions, and analyze performance metrics. Meanwhile, the mobile app empowers citizens to
report emergencies from anywhere—via GPS, typed address, or map pin—and stay informed
with automated SMS/push updates. Field responders use the same app (after role-based login)
to receive assignments, follow turn-by-turn navigation, update status toggles (“En Route,” “On
Scene”), and submit digital checklists or media attachments.
Seamless mobile–web integration is achieved through secure RESTful APIs for core data
operations and WebSocket streams for real-time status and location updates. Offline caching
on the mobile client ensures that incident reports and crew checklists are never lost; once
connectivity returns, all changes automatically reconcile with the web dashboard. SMS fallback
support guarantees non‐app users can confirm locations and receive ETAs, ensuring no one is
left behind.
We chose to develop a companion mobile app because it:
• Addresses necessity: citizens often lack easy hotline access or precise location sharing
tools. The app’s GPS tagging and automated confirmation loops eliminate guesswork
and reduce call-to-dispatch latency.
• Enhances innovation: by blending offline-first design with real-time synchronization, the
system operates reliably in low-connectivity zones—crucial for rural and disasteraffected areas.
• Strengthens collaboration: field crews gain interactive maps, dynamic routing with live
traffic/weather overlays, and instant status reporting, while dispatchers retain strategic
oversight and historical analytics on one unified platform.
This architecture leverages the strengths of both web and mobile worlds—scalable commandand-control on the web, combined with intuitive, always-available reporting and navigation on
mobile—delivering an end-to-end solution tailored to Sri Lanka’s emergency response needs

4.1 Incident Intake & Triage
When an emergency occurs, the first critical step is capturing accurate, complete information.
The Incident Intake & Triage module is the nerve center for all incoming reports. Call Operator at
the Intake console enters caller details and incident notes while viewing geocoded address
suggestions. For SMS reports, the system parses map URLs or plain-text addresses and
presents them for confirmation. Mobile-app submissions arrive with GPS coordinates plus an
optional text description. In every channel, the operator can correct or augment location data:
pinpointing intersections, landmarks, or building footprints. Once validated, the system assigns
a severity-based incident code (e.g., Medical–High, Fire–Medium) and transfers control of the
incident to Dispatcher. Duplicate reports from multiple sources are detected by comparing
geographic proximity and timestamp, then merged under a single incident ID to prevent
redundant dispatches.
Functional Requirements
• Multi-channel intake: web form for call operators, SMS parser, and mobile-app API.
• Interactive geocoding: suggest address candidates and allow manual pin adjustment.
• Incident classification: dropdowns for type (Medical, Fire, Both) and severity levels.
• Duplicate detection: spatial clustering within a pre-specified meter radius and time
window.
• Incident record creation: store caller ID, contact method, location, timestamp, and
status.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Responsiveness: complete intake workflow within a pre-defined time window under
normal load.
• Accuracy: geocoding error margin ≤ 10 meters.
• Usability: operators complete intake with very few screen interactions.
• Reliability: 99.9% availability of intake APIs during peak periods.
Technical Requirements
• Geocoding integration via Google Maps API or OpenStreetMap Nominatim.
• Node.js/Express endpoints accepting JSON payloads for intake and returning geocoded
suggestions.
• MongoDB with GeoJSON fields and a 2dsphere index for spatial queries and duplicate
detection.
• Mongoose schema definitions for incident documents including location coordinates.
• WebSocket channel (Socket.io) to push new-incident notifications instantly to
dispatcher consoles.

4.2 Communications Hub & Citizen Reporting
Once an incident is logged, the Communications Hub manages two-way messaging with
citizens. Immediately after intake, an SMS or push notification is dispatched asking the reporter
to confirm their location (“Reply YES to confirm [parsed address]”). If no reply arrives in 30
seconds, the system flags the incident for a manual callback by the Call Operator. When
citizens receive assignment updates, such as “Ambulance en route (ETA 5 min)”, they can reply
with SOS to escalate or with LOCATION to update their pin. The hub intelligently merges
multiple citizen threads for the same incident, ensuring all participants see status updates. Inapp notifications for registered users and SMS for non-app users guarantee universal coverage.
Fail-safe mechanisms detect broken links or unreadable texts, prompting fallback instructions
to share coordinates or call the hotline.
Functional Requirement
• SMS and push-notification integration for confirmations and status updates.
• Automatic escalation: non-confirmed reports generate dispatcher callbacks.
• Thread merging: group multiple inbound messages under one incident ID.
• Keyword commands: “SOS” to increase priority, “LOCATION” to resend updated
coordinates.
• Fallback prompts: detect invalid URLs and instruct alternative reporting methods.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Throughput: support 200 simultaneous message threads without latency.
• Delivery guarantee: 99.5% message delivery success rate.
• Latency: confirmation and update messages dispatched within 1 second.
• Security: scrub personally identifiable information (PII) in audit logs.
Technical Requirements
• SMS gateway integration (Twilio or Nexmo) with webhook callbacks.
• Pub/Sub message bus (Redis Streams or Apache Kafka) for routing inbound/outbound
messages.
• Node.js microservice handling confirmation logic, retry policies, and dispatcher
escalation.
• React.js UI component for dispatcher message threads with real-time WebSocket
updates.

4.3 Resource Dispatch, Real-Time Tracking & Auto-Reassignment
When an incident is triaged, the system’s dispatch engine selects the closest suitable unit:
ambulance or fire engine, matching required equipment and crew certifications. After the
confirmation from the Dispatcher, the crew’s mobile app receives a push alert to accept or
decline. Upon acceptance, live GPS data streams back to the Dispatcher console, showing
vehicle location, speed, and heading. Status toggles (“En Route,” “On Scene,” “Cleared”) update
both dispatcher and citizen views. If a crew fails to accept within 30 seconds, is stuck in traffic
beyond a threshold, or reports a breakdown, the auto-reassignment logic instantly identifies the
next-best resource and notifies the Dispatcher for the confirmation of reassignment, ensuring
continuous response coverage.
Functional Requirements
• Automated matching of incident codes to nearest available units.
• Accept/decline workflow with 30-second timeout and retry logic.
• Live GPS tracking: location, speed, heading, and updated status.
• Status lifecycle management: en route → arrived → cleared.
• Rule-based auto-reassignment on time-outs, traffic delays, or mechanical failures.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Real-time performance: sub-1-second latency for location and status updates.
• Reliability: 99.5% successful delivery of assignment and reassignment messages.
• Fault tolerance: fall back to SMS notifications if mobile data fails.
• Security: TLS encryption for all location and status data in transit.
Technical Requirements
• WebSocket (Socket.io) server for bidirectional streaming between web and mobile
clients.
• Redis in-memory store for active session state and timeouts.
• Proximity calculations using Haversine formula in dispatch engine (Node.js).
• React Native mobile SDK for crews to send GPS and status, and receive assignments.

4.4 Dynamic Routing & ETA with Traffic/Weather Overlay
Efficient routing can save critical minutes. This module aggregates live traffic feeds: accidents,
road closures, congestion levels and weather advisories such as flood warnings and high-wind
alerts. It computes optimal and alternate routes to an incident location, factoring in current
conditions and historical delay patterns. As conditions evolve, routes are dynamically reoptimized, and crews receive push notifications of detours (E.g.: “Route 5 blocked, rerouting via
Main Street”). The dispatcher map visualizes color-coded traffic lines (green/yellow/red) and
overlays weather hazards (icons for hail, flood zones), enabling proactive decision-making,
such as rerouting through higher-ground roads during heavy rain.
Functional Requirements
• Poll or subscribe to traffic incident feeds and weather alert APIs.
• Compute primary and alternate routes with buffered ETAs.
• Continuous re-optimization on changing conditions.
• Push route-change notifications to crew mobile apps.
• Visual overlay of traffic density and weather hazards on maps.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Performance: initial routing under 3 seconds for up to 50 km paths.
• Accuracy: ETA variance within ±2 minutes of actual travel time
• Availability: API failover mechanisms with 99.9% uptime.
• Usability: clear legends and intuitive map controls.
Technical Requirements
• Third-party Traffic API (TomTom/HERE) and Weather API (OpenWeatherMap).
• Leaflet.js or Mapbox GL for interactive map rendering with custom tile layers.
• Node.js routing microservice with caching in Redis.
• React.js map component integrating route layers and alert icons.

4.5 Vehicle and Crew Registration
Maintaining an up-to-date registry of assets and personnel ensures accurate dispatching and
compliance with regulatory standards. Vehicle Registration captures static data such as make,
model, and plate number. Instead of requiring dedicated GPS modules, each vehicle’s location
is derived from the mobile app on the assigned crew leader’s device. Crew Registration stores
personal details, certifications, contact endpoints (SMS number, email), and role assignments.
New registrations trigger supervisor approval workflows, and changes or deactivations generate
notifications to HR and IT for system updates. Historical records remain immutable for audit
purposes, while active records drive scheduling and dispatch eligibility.
Functional Requirements
• Multi-step registration wizards for vehicles and crew with real-time field validation.
• Supervisor approval queue with email/SMS notifications upon submissions.
• Update and deactivation workflows, using soft-delete patterns to retain audit history.
• Linking crew members to vehicle assignments and their communication endpoints.
• Location tracking via the crew leader’s mobile-app GPS feed rather than separate
hardware modules.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Data Quality: mandatory fields and formats validated in real time.
• Performance: complete any registration submission within 2 seconds under normal
load.
• Security: only users with HR or Admin roles can create, update, or deactivate records.
• Auditability: every change logged immutably for compliance and traceability.
Technical Requirements
• React.js multi-step form components using Yup for schema validation.
• Express.js REST endpoints with JSON Schema validation for incoming payloads.
• MongoDB collections for vehicles, crew, approvals, and change logs; use Mongoose
schemas.
• 2dsphere index on vehicle documents if needed for geospatial queries of leader
locations.
• Mobile-app integration to push periodic GPS updates from the crew leader’s device into
MongoDB.
• Email/SMS notification service (e.g., Twilio SendGrid) to deliver approval alerts.

4.6 Scheduling & Shift Management
To maintain 24/7 coverage, this module lets administrators create reusable shift templates—
defining start/end times, required roles, and headcounts—with support for fixed, rotating, and
on-call patterns. A nightly job then auto-generates or refreshes the master roster for the next
period. All assignments and adjustments are made by designated schedulers through a
streamlined calendar UI featuring drag-and-drop, quick cloning of weekly templates, and colorcoded role views. Validations at creation prevent overlapping shifts, enforce minimum staffing
and rest-time rules, and flag potential overtime. Configurable alerts—via email or in-app
notifications—prompt schedulers when shifts are under-staffed or at risk of overtime, and every
change is captured in an audit log with timestamps and user IDs for full accountability.
Functional Requirements
• Shift template creation and modification (recurrence rules, holiday exceptions)
• Nightly auto-generation of weekly/monthly master rosters from templates
• Manual assignment of crew members and vehicles to generated shifts via calendar UI
• Coverage-rule enforcement on save (minimum staff per role, max hours per week)
• Alerting for under-staffed shifts and overtime threshold breaches
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Scalability: support up to 500 crew members and 200 vehicles without UI lag
• Consistency: atomic updates in the database to prevent conflicting assignments
• Usability: calendar interactions (drag, clone, edit) complete in under 2 seconds
• Auditability: maintain version history for all template and shift document changes
Technical Requirements
• MongoDB collections for shiftTemplates, shifts, and auditLogs
• Node.js/Express.js REST API endpoints for template and shift CRUD
• Mongoose schemas with validation middleware for coverage rules
• node-cron scheduled job to instantiate upcoming shifts nightly
• React.js calendar component (FullCalendar) with drag-and-drop and clone-week
support
• Notification service in Node.js sending emails/SMS when staffing or overtime rules are
breached

4.7 Resource Readiness & Maintenance
Before every shift, field crews complete a digital readiness checklist—inspecting fuel levels, tire
pressure, life-safety gear, and medical kits. Any “fail” marks the vehicle as unavailable and
automatically triggers a maintenance work order. The system tracks mileage and engine hours
to schedule preventive tasks (oil changes, annual certification) and logs corrective repairs when
failures or breakdowns occur during operations. Units overdue for inspections are locked out of
dispatch until a technician signs off. This proactive approach minimizes in-service failures and
ensures that only mission-ready resources enter critical incidents.
Functional Requirements
• Digital pre-shift checklist with pass/fail for each item.
• Auto-generation of maintenance tickets on checklist failures.
• Preventive maintenance scheduling based on mileage/hours thresholds.
• Corrective repair logging with technician start/end timestamps.
• Dispatch lock for units overdue for mandatory inspections.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Offline capability: checklist submission available without connectivity, with automatic
sync.
• Traceability: audit log of every checklist and maintenance event.
• Responsiveness: readiness status updates load within 1 second.
• Security: only certified technicians can complete corrective maintenance forms.
Technical Requirements
• Mobile-app local storage using AsyncStorage or SQLite with bi-directional sync logic.
• Express.js maintenance microservice writing events to MongoDB (using Mongoose
schemas), with an index on timestamps for efficient queries.
• React.js dashboard widget displaying upcoming services and overdue vehicles—data
fetched from MongoDB via REST API.
• Node.js integration module for inventory management to trigger parts procurement
alerts when critical spares run low.

4.8 Post-Incident Reporting
Once an incident concludes, the system compiles a comprehensive document: a timeline of
dispatch, arrival, clearance, and wrap-up timestamps; personnel logs with hours and mileage;
and equipment usage. Crews and supervisors can attach medical forms, or fire containment
reports. Supervisors add free-text annotations to capture unusual challenges: traffic jams,
communication lapses, or environmental hazards. The final report is exportable as a tamperevident PDF or JSON packet for legal audits, insurance claims, or multi-agency reviews involving
police and hospital staff.
Functional Requirements
• Auto-generate incident summary with all status-change timestamps.
• Upload interface supporting document attachments.
• Supervisor annotation fields with structured categories (traffic, comms, best practices).
• Export formats: PDF with digital signatures and JSON for API integration.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Integrity: digital signatures or cryptographic hash to prevent tampering.
• Storage: support up to 50MB of media per incident.
• Performance: report generation under 5 seconds.
• Accessibility: exports conform to ISO metadata and accessibility standards.
Technical Requirements
• Object storage (AWS S3 or Azure Blob) for all media, using presigned URLs for secure
upload/download.
• Node.js PDF generation via PDFKit or Puppeteer, embedding digital signature metadata.
• MongoDB for incident data and audit logs, with a dedicated “exportEvents” collection.
Use Mongoose schemas and an index on export timestamps for fast queries.
• React.js modal components for attachment management and annotation entry,
communicating with backend REST endpoints.
• Integration layer in Node.js to compute cryptographic hashes (e.g., SHA-256) for each
PDF/JSON export and record them in the MongoDB audit collection.

4.10 Role-Based Access Control & Security
To protect sensitive data and ensure compliance, the system enforces fine-grained access
controls. Each user—Call Taker, Dispatcher, Field Crew, Administrator, or Citizen—receives a
tailored interface showing only relevant functions and data. Authentication is handled via single
sign-on, augmented with multi-factor verification. All actions, from incident creation to roster
changes or report exports, are logged in an immutable audit trail. In emergencies, supervisors
can elevate their permissions temporarily; these override events are flagged and require postevent justification.
Functional Requirements
• Role definitions and permission assignments for all user types.
• Single sign-on via OAuth 2.0/OpenID Connect and TOTP-based MFA.
• Audit logging of every CRUD action with user, timestamp, and IP.
• Emergency override workflow: request, approve, and log elevated access.
• Session timeout and forced log-out after inactivity.
Related Non-Functional Requirements
• Security: comply with OWASP ASVS Level 2 standards.
• Availability: 99.99% uptime for authentication services.
• Performance: token validation under 500 ms.
• Privacy: encrypt personal data at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+).
Technical Requirements
• Keycloak or Auth0 for identity management.
• JWT tokens with role-based scopes and short expirations.
• Middleware in Express for permission checks on each API route.
• Separate audit-log database table with write-once retention policies.

4.12 Limitations
While our Emergency Dispatch System is designed to enhance response efficiency and user
experience, it has several inherent limitations that stakeholders should consider:
Dependence on Network Connectivity: The system’s real-time features (map updates, SMS
notifications) require stable internet or cellular coverage. In areas with poor signal, functionality
may degrade, leading to delayed incident reporting or dispatch updates.
Offline Functionality Constraints: Although the mobile app supports basic offline data
capture, full synchronization (e.g., routing adjustments, live status feeds) can only occur once
the device reconnects, potentially creating gaps in situational awareness.
Integration with Legacy Systems: Many dispatch centers rely on proprietary or outdated
software. Bridging our modern, API-driven platform with these legacy tools may require custom
adapters or middleware, introducing additional cost and complexity.
Data Accuracy and Validation: Citizen-reported incidents depend on user input. Without
robust validation controls, reports may contain errors in location or description, necessitating
manual verification by dispatchers and potentially delaying response.
Training and Adoption Barriers: Users accustomed to existing workflows may resist change.
Comprehensive training helps, but initial productivity dips are likely as dispatchers and
responders learn new interfaces and protocols.
Scalability Limits in Extreme Load Scenarios: While designed for typical dispatch volumes,
very large-scale emergencies (hundreds of simultaneous incidents) could strain database
transactions or messaging queues, requiring further tuning or infrastructure scaling.
Security and Privacy Trade-offs: Implementing advanced encryption and strict access
controls protects data but can impact performance on low-powered devices. Balancing security
hardening with usability remains an ongoing challenge.
Maintenance Overhead: Continuous updates to third-party APIs (traffic, weather, SMS) and
dependencies (frameworks, libraries) necessitate regular maintenance cycles, which can divert
resources from feature development.
These limitations frame realistic expectations around system performance, user experience,
and operational readiness. Acknowledging them early enables better planning for mitigations,
alternate workflows, and resource allocation in subsequent phases.

5.1 Sri Lankan Emergency Dispatch Practices
5.1.1 Suwa Seriya Foundation
The 1990 Suwa Seriya Foundation was established by Act of Parliament in 2018 under the
Primary Health Care, Epidemics and COVID Disease Control State Ministry. It operates a fleet of
297 ambulances strategically based in police stations across Sri Lanka, providing free, islandwide pre-hospital emergency care with an average response time of 15 minutes 32 seconds. A
centralized 24/7 command-and-control center monitors a locally developed real-time vehicletracking and navigation system—combining GPS and Google traffic data—to guide EMT crews
along optimal routes. On-call physicians advise EMTs remotely, and every case is followed up
with the patient party within 48 hours. A co-located police post ensures immediate lawenforcement coordination at incident scenes.
Strengths
• Government-mandated service with extensive national coverage
• Real-time vehicle tracking and dynamic routing for reduced call-to-wheel times
• Integrated clinical oversight: EMTs supported by on-call doctors
• 48-hour post-incident follow-up enhances patient care continuity
• Seamless police integration via an on-site emergency liaison
Limitations
• Patient intake still relies partly on manual processes, risking transcription delays
• No unified digital intake form or caller geocoding confirmation loop
• Absence of comprehensive incident history analytics or a tamper-evident audit trail
• Resource scheduling and shift management remain separate from core dispatch
workflows
Necessity for Improvement: To further drive down response times and bolster operational
transparency, Suwa Seriya would benefit from an end-to-end digital dispatch platform featuring
structured intake forms with built-in geocoding, two-way confirmation loops, integrated crew
scheduling, and real-time analytics dashboards.

5.1.2 Sri Lanka Fire Brigade
The Fire Service Department (FSD) of Sri Lanka operates under municipal councils and the
Ministry of Local Government and Provincial Councils, providing fire suppression, rescue, and
hazardous-materials mitigation across all nine provinces. Its origins date back to the Colombo
Fire Brigade, officially formed in 1898, and today the FSD comprises over twenty local
brigades—including Colombo, Kandy, Gampaha, and Jaffna—plus specialized units for airport
and military airfield rescue operations2. Emergency calls route through a 24/7 hotline (110/011-
2422222) to centralized dispatch centers, which coordinate station crews and equipment
deployments.
Strengths
• Extensive geographic coverage via municipal-level brigades and regional sub-stations
• Specialized rescue capabilities (airport fire & rescue units, high-rise ladder trucks)
• Established training and fire-prevention divisions embedded within each council
• Historical resilience, having mechanized operations in the early 20th century and
adapted staffing models (e.g., eight-hour shifts) over decades
Limitations
• Incident intake relies on voice calls and manual logging, introducing delays and
potential transcription errors
• No unified digital dispatch platform or geocoded caller confirmation loop
• Absence of real-time vehicle-tracking and automated ETA updates for field units
• Limited analytics and no tamper-evident audit trail for post-incident review
Necessity for Improvement: To enhance response speed, situational awareness, and
operational transparency, the FSD would benefit from an integrated digital dispatch system
featuring structured intake forms with geocoding, two-way confirmation loops, real-time GPS
tracking, automated routing, and analytics dashboards underpinned by a secure audit log.

5.2 Global Best Practices
5.2.1 Motorola CAPSTAR CAD (North America)
Motorola’s CAPSTAR CAD system integrates call-taking, incident coding, resource allocation,
and real-time AVL (Automatic Vehicle Location) on a GIS map. It features automated mutual-aid
triggers, built-in pre-defined response protocols, and detailed audit logs for compliance.
Strengths:
• Unified digital workflows eliminate manual handoffs.
• Live traffic overlays optimize routing.
• Comprehensive historical database supports after-action review.
Limitations for Sri Lanka:
• High licensing and maintenance costs make CAPSTAR unaffordable for NERCC.
• Requires extensive training and dedicated IT support.
• Proprietary ecosystem limits customization for local infrastructure constraints.
5.2.2 NENA Standards & UK FiReControl
The National Emergency Number Association (NENA) standards in the U.S. prescribe a two-tier
call-taker/dispatcher model with structured incident data exchange and CAD integration. The
UK’s FiReControl project (now replaced by regional CAD hubs) demonstrated centralized fire
dispatch with role separation and GIS-enabled resource tracking.
Strengths:
• Separation of duties reduces errors and improves scalability.
• Standardized data formats enable multi-agency interoperability.
• GIS-driven dashboards offer situational awareness and resource optimization.
Limitations for Sri Lanka:
• Rigid compliance requirements demand heavy IT and operational overhead.
• Centralized UK model assumes robust terrestrial networks unavailable in rural Sri
Lanka.
• Off-the-shelf solutions lack offline-capable mobile reporting features needed in lowconnectivity zones.

5.3 Gap Analysis
Current Sri Lankan emergency dispatch practices span two parallel, largely siloed systems:
Suwa Seriya’s semi-digital ambulance dispatch and the Fire Service Department’s manual firerescue operations. While each has individual strengths, together they expose critical
interoperability, transparency, and efficiency gaps.
Key gaps include:
• No unified digital dispatch platform covering both ambulance and fire services
• Suwa Seriya’s intake still relies partly on manual call logging and transcription, and FSD
intake is entirely voice-call based
• Fire Service Department lacks real-time vehicle tracking and ETA estimation; Suwa
Seriya’s GPS tracking is not integrated with caller confirmation loops
• Neither system incorporates structured digital incident histories, tamper-evident audit
trails, or automated post-incident analytics
• Crew scheduling, shift management, and resource maintenance remain disconnected
from core dispatch workflows
• Absence of offline-capable mobile reporting prevents reliable field updates in lowconnectivity areas
5.4 How the Proposed System Addresses Gaps
Our NERCC Emergency Dispatch System unifies ambulance and fire-rescue operations into a
single, open-source, cloud-native CAD platform built on the MERN stack. It embeds global best
practices—tailored to Sri Lanka’s infrastructure and budget—to close every identified gap.
• Seamless multi-agency dispatch: one platform for call intake, triage, and resource
deployment across EMS and fire brigades
• Digital intake channels: web-based structured forms, SMS and mobile-app options, with
built-in geocoding and two-way confirmation loops to eliminate manual errors
• Real-time situational awareness: GPS tracking of all field units overlaid with live traffic
and weather feeds, plus automated ETA notifications to callers
• Offline-first mobile app: allows field crews and citizens to report incidents and update
statuses even in connectivity-poor zones, with automatic sync once online
• Integrated workforce management: crew scheduling, shift rosters, and equipment
maintenance modules interconnected with dispatch logic
• Centralized analytics and audit trail: real-time dashboards for performance monitoring,
historical reporting, and tamper-evident logs for post-incident review
By leveraging open-source components and a modular architecture, the system delivers
advanced CAD capabilities at a fraction of proprietary licensing costs, while ensuring
adaptability to Sri Lanka’s unique operational landscape.

6. Methodology

6.1. Software Development Life Cycle
We evaluated several traditional and modern SDLC approaches before selecting Agile Kanban
for our Emergency Dispatch System. Below is a brief overview of the alternatives and our
rationale, followed by a summary of our Agile Kanban practices and their benefits.
• Waterfall – Linear, phase-by-phase model with fixed requirements and little room for
change. – Not suitable: our emergency dispatch requirements will evolve as we gather
feedback and refine triage, routing, and mobile integration.
• V-Model – Emphasizes rigorous testing linked to each development phase. – Not
suitable: lacks iterative feedback loops and delays stakeholder validation until late in
the process.
• Spiral – Combines iterative development with risk analysis in each cycle. – Not suitable:
its heavy emphasis on formal risk management and documentation is overkill for a 14-
week academic project.
• Rapid Application Development (RAD) – Centers on fast prototyping and user
feedback. – Not suitable: focuses on early prototypes but does not provide continuous
integration of all modules or flow control across the entire backlog.
• Agile Scrum – Sprint-based, time-boxed iterations with defined roles and ceremonies. –
Not selected: our small team requires more flexible flow and fewer formal ceremonies
than Scrum mandates.
Chosen
We have selected Agile Kanban as our development methodology because it aligns perfectly
with our 14-week schedule and supports the dynamic nature of an emergency response
system. By breaking work into small, manageable tasks and fostering continuous collaboration,
Kanban ensures we deliver high-value features early while maintaining flexibility to adapt to new
requirements.
• Iterative Progress: Breaks development into small, time-boxed tasks that fit our sprint
cadence, allowing steady advancement and predictable deliveries.
• Flexibility: Accommodates mid-stream adjustments—such as refining map overlays or
enhancing SMS fallback logic—without derailing the overall plan.
• Communication: Encourages regular demos, stand-ups, and backlog reviews to keep
dispatchers, developers, and stakeholders aligned and informed.
• Team Coordination: Uses a visual Kanban board and work-in-progress limits to balance
workloads across all five functional areas—dispatch, readiness, registration, intake, and
scheduling.
24
• Incremental Delivery: Enables early demonstrations of core capabilities (incident
intake, basic dispatch), building momentum and securing instructor feedback.
• Quality Management: Integrates continuous testing, review stages, and retrospectives
to identify and address defects promptly, preventing costly rework later.
Agile Kanban Practices
• Backlog Grooming – Bi-weekly sessions to refine user stories, update estimates, and
identify dependencies. Keeps the backlog prioritized and actionable.
• Daily Stand-ups – 15-minute synchronizations where each member shares progress,
plans, and impediments. Ensures transparency and immediate problem-solving.
• Demo to Stakeholders – Every two weeks we deliver a working increment—whether
dispatch intake or routing prototype—for hands-on feedback. Early validation uncovers
hidden requirements and builds stakeholder confidence.
• Retrospectives – After each demo, the team reviews what went well, what could
improve, and commits to action items. Drives continuous process refinement.
• Continuous Kanban Flow – Work items move across a board from “To Do” to “In
Progress,” “Review,” and “Done.” Work-in-Progress (WIP) limits prevent overcommitment and maintain high quality.

Stakeholder Interviews and Workshops: We will conduct structured interviews with NERCC
leadership, dispatchers, call takers, field crews, and selected citizens. These sessions uncover

chosen interviews for richer, real-time clarification.
Use Cases and User Stories: Capturing discrete workflows (e.g., “Report Incident via SMS,”
“Accept Dispatch”) ensures clarity on system behavior. Each user story will include acceptance
criteria for testability. Alternative: Formal SRS document; chosen user stories for agility and
stakeholder engagement.
Prototyping & Wireframing: Low-fidelity wireframes in Figma will visualize key screens—intake
console, dispatcher map, crew app. Iterative feedback refines UI/UX before development.
Alternative: Paper prototypes; chosen digital tools for rapid iteration and remote collaboration.
Prioritization with MoSCoW: Must-have, Should-have, Could-have, Won’t-have categories will
guide feature scope for each sprint, balancing urgency and resource constraints. Alternative:
Kano Model; chosen MoSCoW for simplicity and clear sprint planning

6.2 Design Methods
UML Modeling: Use-case diagrams, sequence diagrams, class diagrams, and component
diagrams will define system structure and interactions. Alternative: ArchiMate; chosen UML for
broad industry acceptance and tool support.
Domain-Driven Design (DDD): We will identify bounded contexts (e.g., Intake, Dispatch,
Analytics) to decompose the system into clear modules. Alternative: Layered architecture;
chosen DDD for better alignment with business domains.
Responsive Wireframes & Interactive Prototypes: High-fidelity prototypes in Figma will
simulate critical workflows (incident intake, dispatch acceptance) to validate usability on both
web and mobile.
6.3 Development Tools & Technologies
Technology Stack: MERN
• Frontend: React.js + Tailwind CSS for component-based UI and utility-first styling.
• Backend: Node.js + Express for lightweight, non-blocking server logic.
• Database: PostgreSQL (spatial extensions via PostGIS) for structured, geospatial
queries; Redis for in-memory caching.
• Mobile: React Native to share code and UI paradigms with web. Alternatives: MEAN
stack or Django + React; MERN chosen for full-JavaScript consistency and large
community support.
Infrastructure & DevOps
• Containerization: Docker with multi-stage builds for consistent environments
• Orchestration: Docker Compose on a single AWS EC2 instance, simplifying deployment
and maintenance
• Infrastructure as Code: Terraform to provision EC2 instance, VPC, subnets, and security
groups; no Kubernetes modules
• CI/CD: GitHub Actions workflows to
o build and push Docker images to a container registry
o SSH into the EC2 host
o run docker-compose pull && docker-compose up -d
• Secrets Management: AWS Systems Manager Parameter Store or Secrets Manager to
store and inject MONGODB_URI and other sensitive settings
Integrations
• Traffic API: TomTom or HERE for live congestion data
• Weather API: OpenWeatherMap for real-time and forecast alerts
• SMS Gateway: Twilio for reliable messaging and webhooks; chosen for generous free
tier, robust SLAs, and JavaScript SDK support

6.4 Testing Methods
Unit Testing: Jest and React Testing Library for JavaScript and React components. Alternative:
Mocha/Chai; Jest chosen for built-in assertions and coverage tools.
Integration Testing: Postman/Newman suites to validate REST API workflows end to end.
Alternative: Supertest; Postman chosen for visual test design and data-driven capabilities.
End-to-End (E2E) Testing: Cypress for simulating user flows on web dashboards and mobile
emulators. Alternative: Selenium; Cypress chosen for faster execution and developer
ergonomics.
Performance & Load Testing: Apache JMeter to simulate concurrent users and measure
response times under stress. Alternative: k6; JMeter chosen for protocol breadth and familiarity.
Security Testing: OWASP ZAP for automated vulnerability scans. Manual penetration tests for
critical endpoints.
6.5 Integration & Deployment Methods
Incremental Feature Branch Integration: Developers work on isolated Git branches. Pull
requests trigger CI pipelines with automated tests before merging to main.
API-First Development OpenAPI (Swagger): specifications define contracts for frontend,
backend, and mobile integrations.
Blue-Green Deployment: Kubernetes deployment strategies ensure zero-downtime releases
and quick rollbacks if needed.
Monitoring & Alerting: Prometheus for metrics collection and Grafana dashboards.
Alertmanager to notify on SLA breaches or error spikes.

7. Evaluation Method
We’ll keep our evaluation straightforward and user-focused, measuring success with simple
checks, feedback, and outcome metrics rather than deep technical audits.
7.1 Functional Testing
• Define key user journeys (e.g., “Report an Incident,” “Accept Dispatch,” “Update
Status”).
• Walk through each scenario with team members and pilot users, marking tasks as
pass/fail.
• Log any issues, group them by severity, and resolve before the next demo.
7.2 User Feedback
Distribute short surveys after stakeholder demos and pilot sessions, asking:
• How easy was it to complete core tasks?
• How satisfied are you with the system on a scale of 1–5?
• What’s the single biggest improvement you’d suggest?
Hold quick debrief interviews with dispatchers and field crews to capture qualitative insights.
7.3 Performance & Reliability
• Track average page or API response times through basic built-in logging.
• Record daily system uptime by checking availability at set intervals.
• Maintain a simple error log (date, description, resolution) to ensure problems are fixed
promptly.
7.4 Usability
• Time how long new users take to complete critical workflows without assistance.
• Observe live or recorded sessions to spot confusing layouts or steps.
• Set a goal: at least 90% of users complete core tasks on their first try.
7.5 Adoption & Impact
• Monitor the number of incidents processed per day and how many are handled end-toend.
• Compare these figures to baseline numbers (if available) to measure efficiency gains.
• Keep an eye on active user counts (dispatchers, responders, citizens) to gauge
adoption.
7.6 Continuous Improvement
• After each demo, consolidate survey scores, test results, and interview notes into a
simple action list.
• Prioritize the top three improvements in the next Kanban cycle.
• Share progress and updated metrics with stakeholders at regular intervals.
